The problem

Simulations are clean. Life is not.

World models learn to predict what happens next. Most of them train on video of human environments or on synthetic physics engines — one is uncontrolled, the other is too tidy. Neither tells you where prediction actually breaks down.

A living organism in a controlled dish sits exactly between the two. The environment is fully specified. The behaviour is not. That gap is the signal we are after.

What we produce

The dataset

Every release ships three layers: raw imaging, derived trajectories, and the full experimental context needed to reproduce them.

Raw imaging

Time-lapse sequences of whole plates under fixed optics and lighting. Nothing cropped, nothing pre-filtered — the frames you train on are the frames we recorded.

Trajectories

Per-individual tracks with position, posture and velocity over time, plus labelled events: reversals, turns, feeding, quiescence.

Experimental context

Strain, age, temperature, food state, plate history and handling log — recorded per run, because a trajectory without conditions is not data.

Technical specification

Subject
Caenorhabditis elegans, wild-type and defined mutant strains from the standard repository

Duration
Full lifespan runs — two to three weeks per cohort, continuous
Scale
Hundreds of individuals in parallel; cohorts repeated independently for replication

Why this organism

The only animal with a complete wiring diagram

For almost every animal, behaviour is a black box: you see the output and guess at the mechanism. For this one, the mechanism is published. Every neuron, every connection, every cell division from egg to adult has been mapped and independently confirmed.

  • Ground truth exists. Predictions made from behaviour can be checked against known anatomy rather than against another model
  • Lifespan fits the experiment. Two to three weeks means a full developmental and ageing arc inside a single recording
  • Statistics are affordable. Hundreds of individuals per run make effect sizes measurable rather than anecdotal
  • No regulatory burden. Invertebrates sit outside laboratory animal legislation worldwide, so nothing stands between a hypothesis and a recording

How we work

Standards we hold ourselves to

A dataset is only worth what its weakest methodological link allows. These are ours.

Conditions travel with the data

Temperature, strain, age, feeding state and handling history are attached to every recording. A trajectory stripped of its context cannot be compared with anything, and we do not ship one.

Replication before release

Nothing is published from a single cohort. Runs are repeated independently, and between-cohort variance is reported alongside the result, not buried in an appendix.

Raw stays raw

Derived tracks are convenient; original frames are the evidence. Both are released, so anyone can rerun the pipeline and get a different answer if ours was wrong.

Failures are recorded

Contaminated plates, tracking losses and discarded runs are logged and counted. A dataset that reports only successful recordings silently overstates its own quality.
