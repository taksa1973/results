The problem

Simulations are clean. Life is not.

World models learn to predict what happens next. Most train on video of human environments or on synthetic physics engines — one is uncontrolled, the other too tidy. Neither shows where prediction actually breaks down.

A model organism in a controlled dish sits exactly between the two. The environment is fully specified. The behaviour is not. That gap is the signal we are after.

plates · ready for imaging

What training data usually looks like

  • Scraped video with no record of the conditions it was shot under
  • Synthetic physics that never surprises the model it trains
  • Clips of seconds, when the behaviour of interest unfolds over days
  • No ground truth — predictions can only be checked against other models
  • Failed and discarded recordings quietly dropped from the release

What we record instead

  • Every frame carries its strain, temperature, age and handling history
  • Real behaviour in a fully specified environment — controlled, not scripted
  • Continuous recording across weeks, not isolated clips
  • An organism whose complete anatomy is published and independently confirmed
  • Contaminated plates and tracking losses logged, counted and published
What we produce

The dataset

Every release ships three layers: raw imaging, derived trajectories, and the full experimental context needed to reproduce them.

Raw imaging

Time-lapse of whole plates under fixed optics and lighting. Nothing cropped, nothing pre-filtered — the frames you train on are the frames we recorded.

Trajectories

Per-individual tracks with position, posture and velocity over time, plus labelled events: reversals, turns, feeding, quiescence.

Experimental context

Strain, age, temperature, food state, plate history and handling log — per run, because a trajectory without conditions is not data.

How it works

One cohort, three stages, nothing hidden in between.

01 — Record

Identical conditions, held for weeks

Hundreds of individuals are plated under one protocol and recorded under fixed optics, fixed light and 20 °C. Every intervention — feeding, transfer, inspection — goes into the log before it goes near the plate.

02 — Track

Automated tracking, verified by hand

Frames become per-individual trajectories: position, posture, velocity, and labelled events. Sampled segments are re-scored manually, and the disagreement rate between the two is published with the data.

03 — Release

Raw and derived, together

A release contains original frames, derived tracks and the full condition set in open formats. Discarded runs are counted in the same document, so the dataset reports its own failure rate rather than hiding it.

Why this organism

The first complete wiring diagram of a nervous system

In almost every model system, behaviour is a black box: you see the output and guess at the mechanism. For this one, the mechanism is published — every neuron, every connection, every cell division from egg to adult, mapped and independently confirmed.

  • Ground truth exists. Predictions from behaviour can be checked against known anatomy rather than against another model
  • The timescale fits the experiment. Two to three weeks of continuous imaging cover an individual's entire history
  • Statistics are affordable. Hundreds of individuals per run make effect sizes measurable rather than anecdotal
  • A standard protocol. Culture conditions have been standardised since 1974, so runs are reproducible across labs

How we work

Standards we hold ourselves to

A dataset is worth only what its weakest methodological link allows. These are ours.

Conditions travel with the data

Temperature, strain, age, feeding state and handling history are attached to every recording. A trajectory stripped of context cannot be compared with anything, and we do not ship one.

Replication before release

Nothing is published from a single cohort. Runs are repeated independently, and between-cohort variance is reported next to the result, not buried in an appendix.

Raw stays raw

Derived tracks are convenient; original frames are the evidence. Both are released, so anyone can rerun the pipeline and get a different answer if ours was wrong.

Failures are recorded

Contaminated plates, tracking losses and discarded runs are logged and counted. A dataset reporting only successful recordings silently overstates its own quality.

Questions

Answers before you ask

What exactly is in a release?

Original frames, per-individual trajectories, labelled behavioural events, and a condition file covering strain, age, temperature, food state and the full handling log. Discarded runs are listed with the reason they were dropped.

Why this organism and not something more complex?

Because the mechanism behind the behaviour is already published. Complexity without ground truth gives you more data and less certainty; here a prediction can be checked against known anatomy rather than against another model.

Can we request specific recording conditions?

Yes — strains, perturbations, temperatures and timescales are configurable. Tell us what your model needs to see, and we will say plainly whether we can hold those conditions long enough to make the recording worth training on.

How do you handle tracking errors?

Sampled segments are re-scored by hand and compared with the automated output. The disagreement rate ships with the dataset, so you can weigh the labels instead of trusting them blindly.

Who can access the data?

Research groups working on world models, representation learning or behavioural prediction, and biology groups that need behaviour quantified at scale. Write to us with what you are building and what you need recorded.
