What we do

ORGANISATION NAME is an independent research organisation working on the cellular and organismal biology of ageing. We run controlled lifespan and healthspan experiments in Caenorhabditis elegans, maintain human fibroblast cultures, and use lentiviral delivery to study how specific genetic interventions change cellular behaviour over time.

We are deliberately small and deliberately slow. The field has no shortage of striking single-subject claims; what it lacks is boring, repeatable measurement. That is the part we are interested in.

Research directions

Three systems, chosen because together they cover the range from whole-organism lifespan to single-cell mechanism — and because each one validates the others.

Lifespan in nematodes

C. elegans lives two to three weeks and produces hundreds of offspring, which makes it the only practical system for running full survival curves with proper statistics. We use it as the primary screen: an intervention that does nothing here rarely earns a place in the cell work.

Human cell cultures

Primary and immortalised fibroblast lines let us watch replicative senescence directly: proliferation rate, morphology, and the point at which a population stops dividing. Cell work answers the question that organism work cannot — what exactly changed inside.

Vector-delivered constructs

Third-generation lentiviral vectors give controlled, stable expression of single genes in culture. This is a laboratory tool for mechanism studies — it is not, and is not intended to be, a clinical intervention.

How we work

Ageing research has a credibility problem, and most of it traces back to method rather than ambition. These are the rules we hold ourselves to.

Controls before claims

Every reported effect comes with a control group, a stated sample size and the measurement noise of the method used. An effect smaller than the error bar of its own assay is not an effect — it is a measurement.

Replication before publication

Nothing leaves the lab on a single run. Survival experiments are repeated across independent cohorts; culture experiments across independent passages.

Raw data stays available

Survival tables, passage counts and imaging data are kept in a form that can be re-analysed by someone else. A result that cannot be recalculated from raw numbers is an opinion.

Negative results count

Interventions that fail are recorded with the same care as those that work. In a field this noisy, a well-documented null result is often worth more than a positive one.

Facilities

A working wet lab built around cell culture and nematode maintenance, operating at biosafety level 2.

Cell culture

  • Class II biosafety cabinet
  • CO₂ incubators with independent backup
  • Refrigerated centrifuge, −80 °C and liquid nitrogen storage
  • Inverted microscope with fluorescence
  • Type I water purification and autoclave

Nematode work

  • Dedicated 20 °C incubator
  • Stereomicroscope station for scoring and transfer
  • NGM plate preparation with E. coli OP50 lawns
  • Strains obtained under standard repository terms

Biosafety and compliance. All vector work is carried out under BSL-2 containment with documented standard operating procedures, written spill and decontamination protocols, and a waste disposal contract with a licensed operator. Equipment is certified on an annual schedule.
